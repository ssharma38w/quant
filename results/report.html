1. Nifty Weekly Options Engine

A systematic options-selling system for NIFTY weekly expiries, targeting 15–20% annual CAGR through premium harvesting (selling IV when it's rich relative to realized vol), combined with regime detection and risk controls.

Key mechanisms

  • Regime detection (HMM) classifies each week as trending, range-bound, or volatile based on price action and IV/HV behavior, driving which options strategy is selected (Iron Condor vs Short Straddle).
  • IV/HV premium harvesting — entries are sized and selected based on implied vol richness relative to realized historical vol.
  • Greeks management — theta decay is monitored as the primary profit driver; delta-neutral hedging keeps directional exposure controlled intra-week.
  • TimesFM integration (src/models/timesfm_forecast.py) — a foundation time-series model forecasts the next 5-day price range. The ratio of predicted_range / VIX-implied range (range_vs_vix) widens or narrows Iron Condor wings, and trades are skipped entirely when the model expects an unusually large move (range_vs_vix > 1.6). Falls back to a VIX-based estimate if the model is unavailable.
  • ML walk-forward validation — a classifier is retrained on a rolling 16-week window to filter/confirm trade entries, avoiding lookahead bias.
  • Stop-loss & risk controls — positions are closed early on adverse moves; stop-loss exit counts are tracked in every report.

3. Lynch-Style Multi-Stock Equity Backtest

A separate module (src/equity/) implements a "One Up On Wall Street" inspired stock-picking strategy across 113 NSE-listed stocks spanning 13 sectors (Banking, Financial Services, IT, Pharma, FMCG, Auto, Energy, Metals, Infra, Telecom, Consumer Durables, Chemicals, Power).

Why factor proxies instead of real fundamentals?

Peter Lynch's categories (Fast Grower, Stalwart, Turnaround, Cyclical) are fundamentally driven (PEG ratio, earnings growth, debt/equity, etc.). yfinance only exposes current-snapshot fundamentals — using them in a historical backtest would be lookahead bias. Instead, each category is approximated with no-lookahead price/volume factors, computed strictly from data available as of each rebalance date:

Lynch CategoryProxy Factors (point-in-time only)
Fast Grower12-1 month price momentum, % of days above 200-day SMA
StalwartLow 60-day annualized volatility, positive 200-day SMA slope, large-cap bonus
Turnaround52-week drawdown ≤ -30% + recent 3-month reversal > +5%
Cyclical252-day rolling beta to Nifty, sector's 3-month relative strength

Each stock gets 4 cross-sectional z-scored category scores; the highest score determines its assigned_category at each monthly rebalance (a stock can migrate between categories over time, e.g. a beaten-down cyclical that mean-reverts becomes a "turnaround").

Backtest engine mechanics

  • Universe: 113 NSE large/mid-cap stocks (src/equity/universe.py, MINI_UNIVERSE = 10-stock smoke test set)
  • Rebalance: monthly (configurable to quarterly), top-20 portfolio
  • Category targets: Fast Grower 35%, Stalwart 30%, Turnaround 20%, Cyclical 15%
  • Weighting: category-balanced equal-weight (or score-weighted, capped at 10%/stock)
  • Costs: 10 bps transaction cost on turnover at each rebalance
  • Benchmark: Nifty buy-and-hold tracked in parallel from the same start capital
  • Liquidity filter: excludes stocks below ₹5 crore 60-day average dollar volume

Live picks mode (separate, caveated)

python main.py --equity --picks generates "what looks interesting today" ideas using current yfinance fundamentals (PEG ratio, debt/equity, earnings growth) filtered per Lynch category. This is explicitly not used in the historical backtest — it's a separate, clearly-labeled idea-generation tool.
